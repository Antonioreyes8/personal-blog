Rave culture is inherently derived from Black and Queer communities facing persecution and discrimination.

Techno was born in the 80s and exploded in the underground scene in places like Detroit. While trans and queer artists were having trouble gaining recognition and breaking through in pop culture, they thrived in these spaces.

Queer collectives built the dance floor and, without its deep-rooted history, the concept would not persist today. Underground Resistance, founded by Mike Banks and Jeff Mills, used techno as a tool of defiance against racism and capitalism.

The globally recognized genre of "house" music literally took its name from where it was born "The Warehouse". A Bronx-born DJ named Frankie Knuckles known as the "Godfather of House," began his legendary residency in Chicago in 1977. He popularized the house style when he moved to Chicago and introduced crowds to a New York style of djing by beat-mixing and layering drum percussion fills over Soul and Disco records.

Understanding this history is important because the story of dance music is deeply intertwined with systemic erasure. Black, Queer, and Trans artists have historically struggled to gain mainstream recognition.

Today collectives throw raves without mindfully giving reverence to the pioneers of the concept. Nevertheless, escaping original intention is impossible as it is engrained in the community to be respectful and loving to everyone as that is what keeps these spaces so attractive.
